Tsavo National Park
— Kenya's Wild Giant

By Kega SafarisJune 28, 20267 min read

Tsavo National Park is one of Africas great wild spaces — vast, untamed, and utterly spectacular. Split into Tsavo East and Tsavo West, together they form one of the largest national parks in the world, covering over 20,000 square kilometres of dramatic landscapes.

Unlike the Maasai Mara where wildlife is concentrated in a small area, Tsavo offers a true wilderness experience. Here you feel the immensity of Africa — the empty red dust plains, ancient lava flows, and skies that stretch to infinity.

⭐ Did you know? Tsavo is home to Kenyas famous red elephants. The animals roll in the parks red volcanic soil, giving them their distinctive rust-coloured appearance — unlike any elephants you will see elsewhere in Africa.

Tsavo East vs Tsavo West

Most visitors choose one side or combine both. Here is the key difference:

Tsavo East

  • Flat, open semi-arid landscape — easier game viewing
  • Famous for large elephant herds and the red dust phenomenon
  • Lugard Falls and the Galana River are key attractions
  • Easier road access from Mombasa — popular for beach and safari combinations

Tsavo West

  • More dramatic terrain — volcanic hills, rivers, and lush vegetation
  • Mzima Springs — crystal-clear natural pools fed by underground water from Kilimanjaro
  • Excellent rhino sanctuary — one of the best places to see black rhino in Kenya
  • Roam Range — hippos, crocodiles, and diverse birdlife

Best Time to Visit Tsavo

Tsavo can be visited year round but the dry seasons from June to October and January to March offer the best game viewing. During the dry months, animals congregate around rivers and waterholes, making them easier to spot in the open landscape.

Tsavo is also excellent during the green season from November to May for birdwatching — over 600 species have been recorded in the park.

Key Wildlife in Tsavo

  • Elephants — the famous red-dust covered herds of Tsavo
  • Lions — Tsavo lions are historically known for being large and occasionally maneless
  • Leopards — frequently seen near the rocky outcrops of Tsavo West
  • Black rhino — protected in the Ngulia Rhino Sanctuary
  • Buffalo, zebra, giraffe, cheetah, wild dog
  • Over 600 bird species including the stunning Tsavo sunbird

Safari Tips for Tsavo

  • Combine East and West — a 5-night split safari gives you the best of both parks
  • Visit Mzima Springs — walk the underwater viewing chamber to watch hippos from below
  • Combine with Mombasa — Tsavo East is only 2 hours from the coast, perfect for a beach-safari combination
  • Pack light neutral colours — the red dust gets everywhere and shows on bright clothing
  • Night drives available — Tsavo offers excellent nocturnal game viewing including leopard, civet, and honey badger
