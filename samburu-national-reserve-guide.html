Samburu National Reserve
— Northern Kenya's Hidden Gem

By Kega SafarisJune 28, 20266 min read

Samburu National Reserve is one of Kenya's most underrated safari destinations — a wild, remote wilderness in the country's dry north that offers something truly unique: the Samburu Special Five. These are five animal species found here that you simply cannot see anywhere else in Kenya.

Set along the banks of the Ewaso Ng'iro River, Samburu's dramatic landscape of acacia woodland, rocky hills and dry riverbeds provides a stunning contrast to the open grasslands of the Maasai Mara.

⭐ The Samburu Special Five: Reticulated giraffe, Grevy's zebra, Beisa oryx, Somali ostrich, and the gerenuk (the giraffe-necked antelope that feeds standing on its hind legs). None of these are found in southern Kenya's parks.

Why Samburu is Special

Beyond the Special Five, Samburu offers excellent Big Five game viewing. Lions are frequently spotted near the river, leopards are surprisingly visible lounging in acacia trees, and large elephant herds come to drink at the Ewaso Ng'iro River daily — creating spectacular photographic opportunities.

The Samburu people themselves add another layer to the experience. The local Samburu community — cousins of the Maasai — are known for their striking red clothing, elaborate beaded jewellery, and traditional way of life. Many camps offer authentic cultural visits.

Best Time to Visit Samburu

Samburu is a year-round destination but the dry seasons from June to October and January to April are best for game viewing. During these months the vegetation is sparse, animals gather at the river, and game drives are most productive.

The reserve receives less rainfall than southern Kenya, making it accessible even during Kenya's "rainy season" when other parks can be muddy and difficult.

Wildlife Highlights

  • Reticulated giraffe — the most beautiful of all giraffe subspecies with a distinctive bold pattern
  • Grevy's zebra — larger than the common zebra with narrower stripes and large rounded ears
  • Gerenuk — the extraordinary long-necked antelope that browses standing upright
  • Elephants — massive herds visit the Ewaso Ng'iro River daily for water
  • Lions and leopards — excellent sightings along the riverine vegetation
  • Crocodiles and hippos — found in the deeper pools of the Ewaso Ng'iro River

Tips for Your Samburu Safari

  • Combine with Maasai Mara — a classic Kenya itinerary combines both for maximum wildlife diversity
  • Stay riverside — lodges and camps on the Ewaso Ng'iro River offer the best wildlife viewing
  • Fly in — Samburu is 350km from Nairobi; a short flight saves 6 hours of road travel
  • Visit the Samburu villages — an authentic cultural experience unlike anywhere else in Kenya
  • Bring a long lens — the open terrain allows for excellent long-distance photography
